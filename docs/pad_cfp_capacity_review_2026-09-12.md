# Pad and CFP optimization when power fluid is constrained

The basic architecture makes engineering sense: model each well at candidate operating conditions, allocate limited water among discrete pump and operating choices, and account for the pressure change that those choices cause. The current implementation is useful for exploring alternatives. It is **not yet a dependable answer to whether adding a well improves total production near a PF limit**. Reproducible cases expose false feasibility, missed combinations, and a default allocation policy that can leave avoidable oil behind.

The highest-value next change is a shared **new-well impact study** with a frozen baseline, explicit operating constraints, and several alternatives evaluated at their own physically consistent pressures. Repair the numerical and feasibility defects before attaching a recommendation to that comparison. Retain the saved oil IPR, measured composition, scoped installed-pump fits, and clean replacement assumptions already established in the well-fit workflow.

This review examines the local application source and deterministic offline probes. Synthetic well responses isolate the allocation and plant algorithms; their rates are not forecasts for actual wells. No production inputs were changed, and the application engines were not modified during this review. The supporting scripts, complete inputs, outputs and source manifest are in the [reproduction folder](pad_cfp_capacity_review_2026-09-12/).

**What is sound in the design**

Per-well response tables combined with constrained allocation are an appropriate foundation. Ellis's original WOFFL project explicitly studied whether an additional well could be supported on an eight-well network, using continuous allocation followed by discrete pump selection. That supports the problem structure; it does not validate this fork's later plant models, pressure coupling or decision search.[^1]

The active resize allocators use all converged candidates, distinguish installed hardware from clean replacement hardware, and allow an explicit economic decision to leave a well off. At a fixed pressure and a stated water price, ordinary small integer cases behave correctly: both adapters agreed with exhaustive enumeration in 120 seeded cases, within the documented numerical comparison tolerance. Solver disagreement is therefore not the principal issue in routine cases.[^2]

The water basis is also deliberately differentiated. S and I allocate lift water; M and E allocate lift plus formation water. This distinction must survive every constraint, marginal comparison and result label. A barrel of PF freed and a barrel of machine throughput freed are not interchangeable on every pad.[^3]

S-Pad already re-solves selected hardware against the station curve and checks a pressure residual. I/M/E have a useful separation between an operator-held header and a limiting pump frontier. CFP's measured-anchor delta approach avoids pretending that a sum of asynchronous well tests equals total measured plant throughput. Each of these is worth retaining, with the corrections below.[^3][^4]

**Pad-by-pad assessment**

| System | Active representation | Engineering assessment | Main restriction on a new-well decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-Pad | Parallel fixed-speed boosters; flow search generates pump selections, followed by station/well pressure closure; lift-water basis | Appropriate shared-pressure formulation. Each fixed selection must settle on the actual curve. | An endpoint failure can discard a valid interior solution. Low-flow operating limits and recirculation need an explicit disposition. Choke/SI strategy is currently rejected for this plant. |
| I-Pad | LP and HP pumps in series, VFD and amp limits; pressure sweep; lift-water basis | Appropriate controlled-header capacity model within the characterized train envelope. | A held-header plan is not the same question as the pressure sag after an unmitigated new-well addition. Minimum-flow and current control-state assumptions need qualification. |
| M-Pad | Parallel HP bank on fixed LP-held suction; pressure sweep; total-water basis | A useful HP-bank model while the LP bank really maintains the assumed suction. | Adding enough load to constrain LP supply invalidates the fixed-suction assumption. Recirculation and low-flow limits require explicit modeling or an operating restriction. |
| E-Pad | Catalog booster/affinity model with selectable build, suction, speed, pressure cap and optional amp cap; total-water basis | Suitable for conditional engineering scenarios after feasibility repairs. | The low-flow branch breaks the assumption that every flow below the maximum budget can reach a header. Several input and envelope-reporting inconsistencies are reproducible. Defaults are not a current field qualification. |
| CFP | Active `cfp_moves` engine: measured discharge anchor, local water-to-pressure slope, per-pad delivered-pressure response tables, discrete move search | Useful for local changes under the stated machine and disposal-control regime. | It is not a complete absolute-capacity or machine-outage model. Pressure-floor clipping, anchor handling and incomplete search can materially change its answer. |

The older absolute-throughput `cfp_optimize.run_joint_optimization` remains in the repository, but the application run endpoint uses `cfp_moves`. Findings or capabilities of the older engine must not be attributed to the active CFP screen.[^4]

General pump-system guidance reinforces the need to check the operating point and individual pump operating limits, rather than just maximum aggregate flow. Parallel machines change the pump curve; their interaction with system demand determines the operating point. Manufacturer minimum-flow requirements remain relevant when allocations or shutdowns reduce demand.[^5]

**Confirmed defects and policy limitations**

The examples below use actual allocation/plant functions with deterministic synthetic response tables. They demonstrate possible algorithm behavior, not the frequency or magnitude of errors on the field fleet.

| Finding | Reproduced behavior | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| E-Pad accepts an undeliverable header | At 4,000 BPD machine flow, a 3,500 psi request is returned as feasible and converged, although the same plant model delivers only 3,229.81 psi. The swept case also chooses 3,500 psi. | A reported oil increase can depend on pressure the represented booster cannot supply. |
| CFP clips an overloaded result into its model range | P0 = 2,800 psi, slope = 15 psi/kBPD, added machine water = 30,000 BPD. The raw equation gives 2,350 psi; a 2,500 psi grid floor produces a feasible result with reported residual zero. | The interpolation boundary acts like an invented pressure-support mechanism. |
| A missing CFP current-pump surface becomes an idle baseline | An online well with its current option absent is anchored at zero oil and water; an alternative becomes a +100 BOPD bring-online action. | Model failure can create an invented production gain. |
| CFP's best plan need not include the best evaluated alternative | One case reports 1,595.4 BOPD versus 1,600 doing nothing. Another reports 1,931.2 although its own single-move board contains a 1,959.4 BOPD result. | The recommended plan can be worse than a known alternative without requiring any debate about a global optimum. |
| Automatic water pricing can reduce feasible oil | With a 1,000 BPD budget, A makes 100 BOPD using 1,000 BPD and B makes 61 using 600. Derived lambda = 0.1 selects B and leaves 400 BPD unused; pure capacity-constrained oil maximization selects A. | The default is not equivalent to maximizing oil under the plant limit. Both solvers correctly solve the priced objective they receive. |
| Greedy choke trimming can overshoot the needed reduction | A makes 500 BOPD using 5,000 BPD; B makes 150 using 1,000. At a 5,000 BPD limit, greedy trimming shuts A and retains 150 BOPD. The discrete optimum shuts B and retains 500. | A locally attractive oil-per-water trim can unnecessarily lose 350 BOPD in this example. |
| Solver failures can look like a valid all-off decision | Injected MILP failure and CP-SAT UNKNOWN both return an empty allocation, the same representation as a successful economic all-off solve. | Downstream code can mislabel numerical failure as an economic shut-in recommendation. |

These results and their full inputs are recorded in the [pad probes](pad_cfp_capacity_review_2026-09-12/pad_probe.json), [CFP probes](pad_cfp_capacity_review_2026-09-12/cfp_probes.json), and [allocation probes](pad_cfp_capacity_review_2026-09-12/allocation_results.json).[^2][^6]

E-Pad's first defect comes from applying only `water <= maximum_flow_at_pressure`. Its frontier is not monotone throughout the low-flow domain: satisfying that upper bound does not prove pressure deliverability. The main sweep ranks candidates without the delivered-header check already present in the separate fixed-plan stress evaluator. Qualification must occur before ranking and must use the actual selected flow, speed/amp limits, header cap and operating mode. If recirculation enables a low-demand operating point, include its flow and constraints explicitly; simply rejecting low demand is not a substitute for modeling a verified recycle arrangement.[^6][^7]

Two additional E-Pad inconsistencies deserve the same repair pass. Individually legal inputs with 3,600 psi suction and a 3,500 psi header cap produce an inverted clamping band and an artificially expanded sweep ceiling, yet the run reports a 3,600 psi feasible header. Cross-field validation should reject the unsupported combination. With a 50 A cap, the envelope can report 60 Hz and 100.32 A even though the frontier calculation itself derates the pump. A physical pressure limit and the displayed speed/amp state must describe the same operating point.[^6]

S-Pad has the opposite risk: a false rejection. In the probe, a selected well uses 40,000 BPD and has a valid station intersection at 3,130.60 psi. Its synthetic response is unavailable above 3,600 psi. The bracket search jumps to the station's zero-flow head, approximately 3,886.9 psi, and rejects the selection when that remote evaluation fails. The run consequently returns an empty plan. Search for a valid bracket within the solved response domain before concluding that no intersection exists. A failed remote pressure solve and a physically impossible plan are different results.[^6]

Low-flow qualification is also insufficiently visible. A three-booster S-Pad probe settles with 20,000 BPD demand, below the model's 22,950 BPD recommended minimum. The result carries `in_range=false`, but the JPCO results panel does not display the corresponding low-flow/recirculation warnings. Surface those conditions for S/M and distinguish net well demand from gross machine flow when a verified recycle arrangement is assumed.[^6]

The CFP floor must be a limit on evidence, not a pressure clamp. Preserve the raw anchored balance. When its solution falls outside sampled/qualified conditions, either extend the response grid within a validated plant range or return an out-of-range result. Keep the upper disposal-control cap as a stated operational assumption, separately from the lower model-domain boundary.[^4]

CFP also needs a coherent anchor contract. `p0_psi` accepts values up to 2,900, while the generated grid ends at the 2,880 psi trip-margin cap. A legal P0 of 2,890 can therefore fail anchoring. Extending the grid alone does not fix it: the current pressure law moves the unchanged baseline to 2,880 while summary reporting still calls 2,890 today's pressure. Validate P0, margin and control mode together, and require the unchanged configuration to reproduce the captured baseline.[^4][^8]

**Why a marginal water price is helpful but insufficient**

For a fixed header, choosing at most one option per well with one water constraint is a multiple-choice knapsack problem. With a deliberate oil-equivalent cost lambda, maximizing `oil - lambda * machine_water` is a legitimate objective. If the purpose is maximum oil subject to an actual capacity, maximize oil under that constraint. A shadow price is then a diagnostic of the resource's value; feeding an automatically derived price back into an already constrained discrete problem changes its objective.

The continuous equal-slope intuition does not guarantee a discrete optimum. A pump cannot generally be operated at 40 percent of a catalog choice, and a shut-in is not an infinitesimal trim. The choke example fails even with simple two-point concave per-well options because the greedy step releases much more water than needed. Use the existing discrete allocator for the choke ladder, with installed hardware held and lower delivered-pressure settings represented as options. A final bounded refill/exchange pass would improve a greedy heuristic, but an exact discrete solve on the sampled ladder is clearer.[^2]

The current automatic price is also derived from adjacent segments of Pareto points rather than a guaranteed concave relaxation. The supporting probe records a nonconcave example. It should be labeled an allocation heuristic estimate, not a certified discrete shadow price. For operator decisions, a more direct metric is the optimized oil difference when usable capacity increases by a stated amount, with the same candidate set and constraints. Discrete responses can be stepwise; report that finite-difference value and its interval.[^2]

Keep the manual lambda option for an engineer who intends to price water beyond the modeled bottleneck. Make the objective visible in the result. Prefer fewer interventions among equal-value solutions, and optionally offer a stated small oil-sacrifice tolerance to reduce changes. Do not silently assign an economic cost or sacrifice production to obtain a tidier plan.

Solver precision and status need separate treatment. The CP-SAT adapter rounds each water demand upward to 0.01 BPD and the total budget downward. This is conservative, but it changes feasibility at exact-fit boundaries. A single 500 BOPD option requiring 1,000.001 BPD against a 1,000.001 BPD budget is accepted by MILP and the exhaustive oracle and rejected by CP-SAT, yielding zero oil. That is a numerical discontinuity, not a meaningful field water margin. Choose and disclose a consistent physical/numerical tolerance, retain conservative resource accounting, and verify final allocations in original units. Do not equate agreement in objective quantization with agreement in capacity rounding.[^2]

Both solver families expose useful status information. CP-SAT distinguishes proven optimum from feasible incumbent and unknown status; SciPy exposes termination status and an optimality bound/gap. Carry those through the result contract. A numerical error, timeout without an incumbent, proven infeasibility, and successful all-off optimum require different outcomes. The label “MILP (exact)” should describe a proven solve of the sampled discrete problem, not a global optimum over nonlinear pressures or a guarantee of field accuracy.[^9][^10]

**CFP must evaluate combinations and control behavior explicitly**

The active CFP engine builds a lambda frontier, ranks single changes and constructs some bring-online-plus-offset pairs. It does not guarantee that the final plan considers the baseline, every single, and every returned pair. A small first improvement is to combine all successfully evaluated alternatives into one candidate pool, deduplicate them, settle them consistently, and select the best admissible result. For an unconstrained improvement run, a feasible baseline must remain a candidate. For a forced new-well run, it is a comparison baseline rather than an admissible final plan.[^4]

The pair generator starts from feasible single moves and limits each side to a shortlist. A new well that fails alone may nevertheless work with an offset. The probe adds a well that drives pressure to 2,740 psi, where it cannot operate; simultaneously shutting another well restores 2,800 psi and gains 200 BOPD. The pair list is empty because the bring-online single was filtered out first. The lambda frontier happens to find this combination in that particular example, which does not repair the missing pair analysis. Generate required-new-well combinations from option identities before testing individual feasibility.[^4]

A seeded three-well oracle found a frontier result 37.47 BOPD below the best enumerated result within the same response tables and pressure domain. This does not establish a fleet-wide error percentage. It establishes that statements in the older methodology that the duality gap is immaterial or that the boards are exhaustive are stronger than the implementation and evidence support. More lambda samples alone cannot guarantee recovery of unsupported discrete combinations. The existing pair filter also requires a pair to outperform each constituent alone; that is unsuitable for a required-new-well study in which an offset-only result does not meet the study requirement.[^6]

A practical solver improvement is to sample discharge, solve a discrete allocation with the corresponding anchored water allowance, and then settle and re-simulate the selected alternatives. Below the disposal cap, the anchored law requires **equality**, within numerical tolerance, between selected water and the flow implied by pressure. A simple upper water bound describes controllable disposal/recirculation only if that control degree of freedom is actually included. At the cap, the stated disposal-retrim assumption permits a different relation. The optimization formulation must specify the operator controls before choosing equalities or inequalities.

The delta model assumes that external loads and the operating regime remain sufficiently stable. Model bias cancels only to the extent that it is shared between the baseline and the changed case. A constant offset can cancel; an incorrect pressure-response slope, pump-change response, or donor-well forecast does not. A new well has no measured before/after response of its own to cancel.

For a local uncapped perturbation, write `a = slope / 1000`, with `W_P` and `O_P` the existing system's water and oil pressure derivatives. Differentiating the anchored equation gives:

```
change in pressure = -a * direct change in machine water / (1 + a * W_P)
change in total oil = direct oil change + O_P * change in pressure
```

This explains why the relevant comparison is total-system gain. It also shows which quantities require field validation: direct water burden, pressure response of the supply system, and oil response of the existing wells. At a control or trip boundary, this local derivative does not describe both sides of the transition.

Near the PF limit, distinguish at least two actual control regimes: spare control capacity that can hold the requested pressure, and exhausted control capacity where added demand lowers pressure. The current CFP slope model is a conditional local approximation. A single regression slope cannot establish both regimes, machine-out behavior, disposal headroom and absolute maximum capacity. A future machine-count scenario needs separately qualified machine/control information; the active run does not acquire that merely because legacy plant code has a machine-count interface.[^4]

**The comparison the operator needs**

Capture one study snapshot: dated online state, installed hardware, saved model versions, WC/GOR, simultaneous plant and pad pressures, booster configuration, supply/return constraints, and the new well's explicit assumptions. Compare every alternative against that same snapshot.

| Alternative | What stays fixed or is allowed | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| Existing operation | Actual online wells and hardware; captured control regime | What is the modeled baseline, and how well does it match the measured operating point? |
| Best existing operation, without the new well | Same allowed changes and operating constraints used for the expansion case | How much optimization opportunity already exists without adding a well? |
| Add the well, hold existing hardware and operating settings | New well required online; chosen new hardware; solve shared-pressure consequences | Can it be added as proposed, and what happens to existing production? |
| Add the well, permit choke changes | Existing hardware held; only explicitly controllable throttles; new well required online | Can operating changes accommodate it without pump work? |
| Add the well, permit a bounded number of downsizes or replacements | New well required online; protected wells stay on; catalog/inventory and intervention limits enforced | Which specific changes create room at least oil cost? |
| Add the well, permit selected shut-ins | Explicitly eligible wells may shut; protected wells stay on | Which shutdown combination gives the best total result? |
| Add the well with another qualified booster/control configuration | All preceding well restrictions preserved | Is additional supply capability worth considering versus reallocating current capacity? |

“Hold settings” needs an explicit meaning: fixed choke position is different from holding PF rate or actively holding each well's delivered pressure. State the available control variable and its limits. A plan with lower delivered pressure requires a feasible valve action and cannot be treated as a fractional pump.

For each alternative, show new-well oil, existing-well oil change, total oil change, PF draw, formation water, each constrained machine stream, shared and delivered pressures, resource headroom, operating violations, and the required actions. Negative results and failed operating points must remain visible. Report both:

```
Operating gain = oil with the proposed plan - oil in existing operation
Expansion benefit = best oil with the new well - best oil without the new well
```

The second comparison prevents attributing an improvement on existing wells to the new well. A gross 300 BOPD new-well forecast is not a 300 BOPD pad benefit if the shared pressure change or offsets cost 200 BOPD elsewhere. These example numbers illustrate accounting only.

The existing “Current pumps at plan header” comparison remains useful for isolating hardware effects, and it is correctly labeled as a counterfactual. It does not establish the current configuration's own settled pressure or feasibility and cannot answer the full expansion question. The current I/M/E stress feature likewise compares two fixed plans at shared imposed headers; it does not yet settle a new-well study across S-Pad or CFP control states.[^7]

Do not reuse the older `evaluate_fixed_scenario` or `evaluate_existing_scenario` helpers unchanged for this study. They retain PF-only coupling where M/E require total water and allow fallback substitution or measured-rate adjustments. No active server callers were found in this review. A strict new-well comparison must hold the specified choices and report unavailable operating points explicitly.[^3]

**Workflow gaps that obstruct that study**

The request schema permits a future well's name, destination pad and donor. It has no required-online flag, explicit planned pump, well-level shutdown eligibility, hardware locks or intervention budget. Pad-offline wells are removed; CFP-offline wells become bring-online candidates. The same checkbox therefore carries different operational consequences. Availability today and permission to start or stop a well must be separate concepts.[^8]

Future wells cannot currently be analyzed meaningfully in the hold-pumps choke strategy because they have no tracked installed pump to hold. Add a planned new-well pump separately while retaining the installed identity of every existing well. A donor is a starting model, not a claim that a new well shares the donor's productivity, geometry, composition or backpressure. Allow explicit scenario overrides without editing the donor's database record. Any new-well IPR alternative should be an explicit study assumption; existing wells retain their approved curves.

Preserve the installed option even when an engineer narrows the replacement catalog, unless a change is explicitly required. `scoped_pumps` currently includes installed hardware only if its size is within the requested sweep. Excluding a nozzle/throat from the grid can thus unintentionally remove the no-change option. CFP unions tracked sizes into its grid, but pad resize runs do not guarantee that same behavior.[^11]

The CFP UI renders the plan and single-move boards but does not render the returned pair list. It also initializes “PW discharge today” to a fixed 2,792 psi, while the server can obtain current pad PF clusters. Those values need a common timestamp or a clearly stated manual scenario anchor. The “Plant water” card currently shows modeled water from the run wells; it should not be read as measured total CFP throughput.[^8]

A smaller but concrete identity defect should be fixed before expanding future-well scenarios. A future label matching an existing well is accepted. Hydration then contains two configurations with the same identity, and the coverage ledger overwrites that existing well's role as `future`. Duplicate labels across destination pads are also accepted by the schema. Validate globally unique study identities and reject collisions before building configurations. The [workflow probe](pad_cfp_capacity_review_2026-09-12/workflow_probe.json) reproduces both acceptance paths with database access replaced by local fixtures.

A valid CFP resize can also fail during API enrichment: if the old pump cannot operate at the new settled pressure, indexing its unavailable water result raises a TypeError. The candidate's old state belongs at the baseline pressure when reporting total before/after change. A same-pressure hardware-only diagnostic can instead be unknown. Do not discard a valid new operating point to manufacture the old one.[^8]

**Physical scope and evidence needed for reliable rankings**

The pad models assign a common supply pressure to modeled wells, while saved wellhead return pressure remains a fixed boundary. CFP transfers changes in discharge to responsive pads using measured pressure offsets or fixed line-loss assumptions. These are reasonable simplifications over a qualified operating range, but a substantial new well changes flows in supply and return lines. If those changes materially alter pressure losses or production backpressure, the model needs that coupling or a measured bound on its impact.[^3][^4]

Published production-network formulations explicitly couple per-well responses to manifold flow balances, pipe pressure losses and phase-capacity constraints. That supports adding the relevant network boundaries as the study scope grows. It does not imply that a large general network solver is required before a useful local study can be built.[^12]

Confirm routing before claiming a pad optimum is a field optimum. The request validator excludes all POPS pads from CFP on the assertion that none of their water rides the machines. Other repository documentation says I/H/S lift-water separation allows formation water to reach the central facility. Arrival at the facility does not establish throughput through the PF machines; central disposal can split the stream. This is a topology and accounting question requiring the actual routing/control basis, not justification to add every pad's total water to CFP.[^13]

Use separate constraints for the streams each piece of equipment handles. A future integrated model can express a routing matrix multiplying PF and formation-water contributions, with independently known support/disposal loads and pressure-dependent capacities. Current independent pad optimizations are conditional local optima; they do not jointly enforce every pad, plant and return-system limit.

The saved well curve should stay fixed while evaluating pump, pressure, WC and GOR alternatives. Historical qualification should use each test's measured composition and hold out later operating events or pump installations. The decision metrics are change in oil, change in PF, pressure response and the ability to operate across the candidate range. Matching BHP alone, or agreeing between allocators fed the same biased response tables, cannot establish those quantities.

The choke evidence layer currently adjusts suction/oil response while retaining modeled PF. This is an empirical correction with provenance, not a fresh simultaneous solution of all pump and return-flow balances. Keep corrected and pure-model evidence distinguishable, restrict empirical extrapolation to supported conditions, and recheck the corrected candidate's water and operating limits. Existing fit/replay qualification work is relevant evidence, but it does not validate every pad's marginal response.[^14]

For uncertainty, compare the same fixed alternatives across stated WC/GOR, pressure, plant-slope and new-well productivity assumptions. Also reoptimize selected cases to measure how much could be recovered by changing the plan. Those are different questions: fixed-plan sensitivity measures exposure; reoptimization measures flexibility. Record infeasible and unknown cases instead of dropping them from a favorable oil range. Rank alternatives by admissibility first, then the stated objective, with action burden and model support shown separately. Scenario counts are not probabilities.

**Implementation order and acceptance checks**

1. **Repair result validity.** Enforce E deliverability and valid pressure windows; retain raw CFP pressure residuals; reject missing online anchors; preserve solver status; keep valid baselines in the candidate pool; fix unavailable-old-option handling. Every selected plan must pass pressure, capacity, identity, domain and coverage checks before ranking. No failed model or failed allocation can become a zero-rate economic action.
2. **Make the discrete comparison consistent.** Offer capacity-constrained maximum oil explicitly; retain deliberately priced water as a separate objective. Use one allocator for sampled resize and choke options. Preserve installed choices, add per-well action restrictions and required-online new wells, and check all retained single/pair alternatives. Test numeric boundaries and expose sampled-problem optimality status.
3. **Build the new-well impact study.** Freeze the shared snapshot and run the comparison table above. Display net existing-well effects, expansion benefit, remaining headroom, and a short list of feasible alternatives with action counts. Include paired offsets in CFP results. Make running offline status independent of allowed actions.
4. **Qualify field and control response.** Reconstruct matched operating events with machine state, disposal/control context, measured pad pressure, actual test WC/GOR and pump identity. Validate withheld pressure changes, pump downsizes and well additions. Confirm routing and supply/return loss assumptions before extending local claims to the full field.
5. **Add coupled stress and facility alternatives.** Extend fixed-plan evaluation to S and CFP, then add qualified machine-out/capacity cases and broader network constraints. Adaptively refine response grids around feasibility knees and competing plans. Re-simulate finalists at their final pressures through the existing shared pool/cache.

The acceptance suite should include unchanged-operation invariance; nonnegative optimum improvement when optional choices are added under the same objective; required-new-well infeasibility without silently dropping it; a new-well/offset pair feasible only jointly; exact-fit capacity boundaries; valid roots inside failed outer samples; lower and upper pressure-domain violations; correct stream accounting; and an oracle check that no reported best plan is worse than its own feasible baseline, single or paired alternatives.

These changes fit the current architecture. Small discrete solves and candidate comparisons can reuse cached response tables; they do not justify a larger compute tier. Keep the Medium worker/job/thread limits and perform expensive physics only for missing response points and shortlisted operating plans.
